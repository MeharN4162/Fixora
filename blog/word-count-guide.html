How Long Should Your Essay, Resume, or Blog Post Be?

🔤

On This Page

  • Essays
  • Resumes & Cover Letters
  • Blog Posts
  • Social Media Captions
  • Checking Your Own Word Count

"How long should this be?" is one of the most common writing questions — and the honest answer is always "it depends." Here are practical, real-world benchmarks for the most common types of writing.

Essays

  • High school essay: 300–1,000 words
  • College application essay: 500–650 words (many platforms cap this strictly)
  • University essay/paper: 1,500–3,000 words, depending on the assignment
  • Research paper: 4,000+ words, following your instructor's requirements

Always follow the specific word limit given — going significantly over or under a stated limit is one of the most common (and easily avoidable) mistakes.

Resumes & Cover Letters

  • Resume: Aim for one page (roughly 400–600 words) for under 10 years of experience; two pages max otherwise.
  • Cover letter: 250–400 words — long enough to make your case, short enough to actually get read.
Tip: Recruiters often spend under 10 seconds on a first resume scan. Concise, scannable writing beats a longer resume every time.

Blog Posts

  • Quick news/update post: 300–600 words
  • Standard how-to or listicle: 800–1,500 words
  • In-depth guide (like this one): 1,500–2,500+ words

Search engines don't reward length directly — they reward content that fully answers the reader's question. Write until you've covered the topic well, then stop.

Social Media Captions

  • Twitter/X: 280 character limit (roughly 40–50 words)
  • Instagram caption: First 125 characters show before "more" — keep the hook short
  • LinkedIn post: 150–300 words performs best for engagement
