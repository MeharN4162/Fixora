How to Compress a PDF Without Losing Quality

A PDF that won't attach to an email, or takes forever to upload to a job portal, is one of the most common everyday tech annoyances. The good news: most PDFs can be shrunk dramatically without becoming blurry or unreadable — you just need to understand what's actually taking up the space.

Why PDFs Get Large

PDFs aren't inherently heavy — text and vector graphics take up very little space. The vast majority of oversized PDFs are large because of one thing: embedded images. This is especially true for:

  • Scanned documents (each scanned page is essentially a large photo)
  • PDFs exported from PowerPoint or Canva with high-resolution images
  • Documents with screenshots pasted in at full resolution

A 20-page scanned contract, for example, might contain twenty full-resolution photos stitched together — easily 15–40MB, even though the actual content is just text.

Before You Compress

Compression always involves a trade-off between file size and quality. Before you compress, ask yourself:

  • Does this need to be printed? If yes, keep quality higher.
  • Is this just for on-screen viewing or email? You can compress much more aggressively.
  • Does the text need to stay selectable/searchable? Image-based compression flattens pages into pictures, so text is no longer selectable afterward — keep an uncompressed copy if you need the original later.
Tip: Always keep your original file. Compression is one-directional — once quality is reduced, you can't get it back.

How to Compress a PDF

  1. Open our free PDF Compressor tool.
  2. Upload your PDF — it's processed entirely in your browser, so nothing is uploaded to a server.
  3. Choose a compression level: High, Balanced, or Low.
  4. Click Compress PDF and wait a few seconds while each page is optimized.
  5. Download your smaller file and compare the size reduction shown on screen.

Choosing a Compression Level

High compression gives you the smallest possible file — ideal for emailing scanned forms or quick reference documents where pixel-perfect quality doesn't matter.

Balanced is the best default for most people — noticeably smaller files while keeping text and images crisp enough to read comfortably on screen.

Low compression keeps quality closest to the original — use this if the PDF contains detailed photos, diagrams, or will be printed.

Tips for Better Results

  • If a PDF was created by scanning, scan at a lower DPI (150–200) next time instead of compressing after the fact.
  • Remove unnecessary pages before compressing — fewer pages means a smaller file, always.
  • If your PDF is mostly text with no images, it's likely already small — compression won't help much (and that's normal).

FAQ

Is this actually free? Yes — the tool runs entirely in your browser using open-source PDF libraries, so there's no server cost to pass on, and no limit on how many files you compress.

Does my file get uploaded anywhere? No. Everything happens locally on your device. Your PDF never leaves your browser.

Will the text still be selectable after compressing? No — our compressor works by re-rendering each page as an optimized image, which is the most reliable way to shrink scanned or image-heavy PDFs. If you need selectable text preserved, only compress a copy.
