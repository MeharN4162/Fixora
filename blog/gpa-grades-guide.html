How GPA and Weighted Grades Actually Work

GPA and course grades both come from converting raw scores or letters into a standardized number — the exact conversion rules just aren't always obvious.

The Standard 4.0 Scale

Each letter grade maps to a point value: A = 4.0, B = 3.0, C = 2.0, D = 1.0, F = 0.0, with plus/minus grades typically adjusting by a fraction like 0.3. GPA averages these point values across all courses, usually weighted by each course's credit hours, so a 4-credit class counts more toward the total than a 1-credit one.

Weighted vs. Unweighted GPA

Unweighted GPA treats every class the same regardless of difficulty, topping out at 4.0 for a perfect record. Weighted GPA adds extra points for harder classes — Honors or AP courses commonly add 0.5 to 1.0 on top of the standard scale — which is why some students report a GPA above 4.0: it reflects the additional difficulty of the courses taken, not a different scoring scale entirely.

Tip: When comparing GPAs between schools or students, check whether both are weighted or unweighted the same way — a 4.3 weighted GPA and a 4.0 unweighted GPA aren't directly comparable numbers.

The "Drop the Lowest Score" Rule

This rule excludes your single worst assignment or quiz score from the average entirely before the final grade is calculated, cushioning the impact of one bad day, missed assignment, or off week. It typically drops only one score (or however many the syllabus specifies) — not every below-average score, just the single worst one.

Figuring Out What You Need on a Final

Work backward from the target grade using the final exam's weight: subtract your current weighted score (everything except the final) from the target overall grade, then divide by the final's percentage weight. The result is the minimum score needed on the final alone to hit that target, assuming everything else stays as already recorded.

FAQ

What's the difference between weighted and unweighted GPA? Unweighted GPA treats every class equally regardless of difficulty, capping out at 4.0 for a straight-A record.
