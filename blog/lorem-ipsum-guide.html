"Lorem ipsum dolor sit amet..." is instantly recognizable to anyone who's touched design software, but its actual origin and purpose are less well known than the text itself.

Where It Actually Comes From

The text is genuine Latin, but scrambled — it's derived from a passage in Cicero's philosophical work "De Finibus Bonorum et Malorum," written around 45 BC, with words rearranged, fragments cut, and some Latin-sounding filler added. It isn't a coherent sentence in classical Latin, but it isn't invented gibberish either — it has real, traceable roots in an actual ancient text.

Why Not Just Use Real English?

Readable English pulls a viewer's focus toward the actual words and their meaning — exactly the wrong thing when the point of a mockup is to evaluate layout, spacing, and typography, not content. Lorem Ipsum's Latin-like but meaningless text lets the eye register realistic word length, sentence rhythm, and paragraph shape without a reader getting hooked on content that isn't final anyway.

Tip: If a headline or button label has a strict character limit, generate placeholder text at roughly that length rather than a generic paragraph — it stress-tests the layout under realistic conditions instead of an arbitrary block of filler.

When Lorem Ipsum Is the Wrong Choice

When the actual content's length, tone, or structure genuinely matters to a design decision — a client reviewing real product copy, or a layout that needs to fit specific real headlines — generic placeholder text can hide real problems, like a headline too long for its container once actual content goes in. In those cases, real or realistic draft copy serves the review better than Latin filler.

The Business Jargon Variant

Some generators offer a "business jargon" style as a playful alternative — swapping Latin-sounding words for corporate buzzwords like "leverage," "synergy," and "actionable" while keeping the same meaningless, placeholder purpose. It's purely a style choice for contexts where business-flavored filler reads more naturally in the layout than Latin.
