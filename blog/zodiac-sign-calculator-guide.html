Western Zodiac Signs and Dates Explained

Everyone knows their zodiac sign, but far fewer people know why the date ranges are set where they are, or why two horoscope sites will occasionally disagree by a day about which sign a given birthday falls under. Both come down to a bit of astronomical bookkeeping most horoscopes gloss over.

The Tropical Zodiac, Not the Stars Themselves

The Western zodiac used by most calendars and horoscopes is the tropical zodiac, which divides the year into 12 equal segments based on the sun's apparent position relative to Earth's seasons — not on where the actual constellations currently sit in the sky. Because of a slow wobble in Earth's axis called precession, the tropical zodiac's sign boundaries have drifted out of alignment with the constellations they were originally named after, by roughly a full sign's worth over the last two thousand years. This is a known, well-documented divergence, and it's why the tropical system (which is what date-based zodiac calculators use) and the constellations overhead on any given night are no longer the same thing.

Why Date Ranges Shift Between Sources

Each sign boundary corresponds to a precise moment the sun crosses a specific point on the ecliptic (its apparent yearly path), and that exact moment shifts by up to about a day from year to year because a calendar year isn't a perfectly round number of Earth rotations. Most horoscope sites round that boundary to a fixed calendar date for simplicity, but since the true crossing moment can land on either side of midnight depending on the year, sources occasionally disagree by a single day on where a boundary officially falls.

Tip: If your birthday lands within a day or two of a listed boundary date, don't assume every source will agree — check the exact year if it matters, since the true astronomical boundary does shift slightly year to year even though most published date ranges don't.

What Being "Born on the Cusp" Means

"Born on the cusp" is popular shorthand for a birthday that falls close to one of these boundary dates — within a day or two of the sign switching over. It's a real, common talking point, but it isn't a third official sign or a blend of two signs in the tropical system itself: any specific birth date and year, precisely calculated, still falls under exactly one sign. The "cusp" language mostly reflects how close that calculation runs, and how easy it is to misremember which side of a boundary a birthday landed on.

How This Differs From the Chinese Zodiac

It's easy to conflate the two, but they're unrelated systems. The Western zodiac assigns one of 12 signs based on the sun's position on an exact birth date within a single year, cycling through all 12 signs every year. The Chinese zodiac instead assigns one of 12 animals to an entire birth year, cycling through all 12 animals once every 12 years — so your Western sign changes every year based on your birthday, while your Chinese zodiac animal is fixed for life based on your birth year.

FAQ

What dates are used for each zodiac sign? This tool uses the standard tropical Western zodiac date ranges, for example Aries runs March 21 to April 19 and Leo runs July 23 to August 22. These are the conventional date ranges used by most Western horoscopes and calendars.

Why do some sources give slightly different date ranges? The exact boundary date between two signs can shift by a day depending on the source, since it depends on the precise time of the sun's position each year. If your birthday falls right on a boundary date, different sources may occasionally disagree by one day.

What does it mean to be "born on the cusp"? Being "on the cusp" just means a birthday falls very close to the boundary between two adjacent signs, within a day or two of the date range switching over. It's a popular way of describing that near-boundary timing, not a third official sign or a blend of the two — a given birth date and year still falls under exactly one sign in a fixed tropical calendar.

Is this the same as the Chinese zodiac? No — the Western zodiac is based on the sun's position on your exact birth date and has 12 signs tied to specific date ranges within a year. The Chinese zodiac instead assigns one of 12 animals to your entire birth year, which is a completely different system.
