How to Merge, Split, and Reorder PDF Pages

📎

On This Page

  • Reordering Pages Without Starting Over

Most people don't create a PDF from scratch — they end up with one that was scanned, exported, or emailed to them, and then they need to fix it. Maybe two contracts need to become one file, or a 40-page scan needs to become just the 3 pages someone actually asked for. These are page-organization problems, not compression problems, and they each have a dedicated tool.

Combining Multiple PDFs Into One

This is the most common request: you have a cover letter, a resume, and a portfolio as three separate PDFs, and a job application form that only accepts a single upload. Merging solves this without needing any desktop software.

  1. Open the PDF Merger tool.
  2. Upload the PDFs you want to combine — you can add more than two.
  3. Arrange them in the order you want the final document to read.
  4. Download the single merged file.

Because the merge just concatenates each file's existing pages, nothing about the original formatting, fonts, or embedded images changes — the output is exactly what you'd get from physically stapling the documents together in order.

Removing Pages You Don't Need

If a PDF has a few pages you want gone — a blank cover sheet, an outdated page, a duplicate — PDF Splitter handles this in Remove mode. Every page renders as a thumbnail; click the ones you want gone, and the tool rebuilds a new PDF from everything else.

Extracting Just the Pages You Need

Sometimes the more useful direction is the opposite: out of a 40-page scanned packet, you only need pages 12–14. Switch PDF Splitter to Extract mode, click only the pages you want to keep, and it builds a new PDF from just those — everything else is left out.

Tip: Remove mode and Extract mode solve the same underlying problem from opposite directions. If you're only keeping 2 out of 40 pages, Extract mode means fewer clicks than clicking 38 pages to remove.

Reordering Pages Without Starting Over

If a scanner fed pages in the wrong order, or a merged document needs its sections rearranged, you don't need to split and re-merge manually. The Reorder PDF Pages tool shows every page as a thumbnail with up/down controls, so you can move pages into the correct order directly and save the result as a new file.

A Common Real-World Workflow

These tools are often used together. A typical sequence: merge a resume and cover letter into one file, notice the cover letter ended up after the resume instead of before it, reorder the pages so the cover letter comes first, then extract just those two pages if a portfolio PDF got merged in by mistake. Each step takes under a minute since none of it requires re-uploading to a different service — you can chain the tools as needed.

FAQ

Will merging PDFs change the formatting of my documents? No — each PDF's pages are copied over exactly as they are, formatting, fonts, and images included. Merging just combines the pages of multiple files into one document in the order you choose.

What's the difference between removing pages and extracting pages? Removing pages keeps everything except the ones you select. Extracting pages does the opposite — it keeps only the pages you select and discards the rest. Fixoona's PDF Splitter supports both, with a toggle to switch modes.

Can I reorder pages without re-uploading the whole document? Yes — the Reorder PDF Pages tool shows a thumbnail of every page with up/down controls, so you can rearrange the existing file directly without splitting and re-merging it yourself.

Is there a limit to how many PDFs I can merge? There's no hard limit set by the tool, but since everything processes in your browser's memory, very large numbers of large PDFs may slow down depending on your device.
