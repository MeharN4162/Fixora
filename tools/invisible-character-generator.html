Invisible Character Generator

Generate real zero-width and blank Unicode characters to copy and paste anywhere you need invisible text.

How to Generate Invisible Characters

  1. Choose which invisible character you want from the dropdown (Zero Width Space, ZWNJ, ZWJ, Word Joiner, Hangul Filler, or Braille Pattern Blank).
  2. Set how many copies you need with the number field (1–50).
  3. The output field updates instantly with your generated string.
  4. Click "Copy" to copy it to your clipboard.
  5. Paste it wherever you need blank-looking space.

Frequently Asked Questions

Will these invisible characters bypass a website's character limit?

No — don't count on that. Zero-width and blank characters still count as characters toward almost every platform's length limit; they just don't display visible ink. This tool is for cases where you want blank-looking space, not for stretching character limits.

Will every platform accept zero-width characters?

Not always. Many chat apps, usernames, and form fields strip zero-width characters automatically, and some validators reject them outright as suspicious or invalid input. Test on the specific platform you're targeting before relying on it.

What's the difference between the zero-width options and Hangul Filler or Braille Pattern Blank?

Zero Width Space, Zero Width Non-Joiner, Zero Width Joiner, and Word Joiner are true zero-width characters — they take up no visible width at all. Hangul Filler and Braille Pattern Blank instead render as a small blank-looking glyph with actual width, which is why they're often used for things like a 'blank' username where a field requires at least one visibly-occupied character.

Why would I want an invisible character at all?

Common uses include blank social media display names, spacing out text in places that strip leading or trailing whitespace, or working around form fields that reject a completely empty value.

Is this guaranteed to work for blank usernames on every app?

No guarantee — platforms change their filtering rules over time, and what works today may be blocked tomorrow. Treat this as a starting point to experiment with, not a guaranteed trick.
