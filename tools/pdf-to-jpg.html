How to Convert a PDF to JPG

  1. Upload your PDF.
  2. Optionally enter specific pages (e.g. "1-3, 5, 8-10") — leave blank to convert every page.
  3. Pick a Resolution (1x-3x) and adjust the Image Quality slider (40-100%) to balance sharpness against file size.
  4. Click "Convert to JPG" — each selected page renders as a thumbnail with its own Download button.
  5. Use "Download All Pages" to save every converted page at once, or download individual pages one at a time.

Frequently Asked Questions

What resolution are the JPGs?

It depends on the Resolution setting you pick before converting — 1x, 1.5x, 2x (default), or 3x scale. At the default 2x scale, a standard Letter/A4 page produces roughly 1600-1700px wide images; higher scales produce larger, sharper images with correspondingly bigger file sizes.

Can I convert only specific pages instead of the whole PDF?

Yes — use the Pages field to enter specific page numbers and ranges, like "1-3, 5, 8-10", and only those pages are converted. Leave it blank to convert every page.

What does the Image Quality slider actually control?

It sets the JPEG compression quality (40-100%) used when each page canvas is exported. Lower values produce smaller files with more compression artifacts around text and lines; higher values keep detail sharper but produce larger files.

Why did "Download All Pages" only save some of my pages, or trigger a browser prompt?

Downloading many files at once triggers your browser's multi-file download permission — the tool downloads pages with a short delay between each to avoid overwhelming the browser, but if your browser blocks multiple downloads you may need to allow them, or just download pages individually.

Can I convert a password-protected PDF?

No — if the PDF can't be read directly, conversion will fail with an error asking you to try a different file. Remove the password with your PDF viewer first.

Will this preserve transparency or vector sharpness?

No. Each page is flattened onto a white background canvas and exported as a JPEG, which doesn't support transparency; for lossless output that better preserves fine text edges, use the PDF to PNG tool instead.
